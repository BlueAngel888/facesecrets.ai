## What others read in 100 milliseconds

People form impressions of trustworthiness, competence and warmth from a face in about a tenth of a second (Willis and Todorov, 2006). These impressions are surprisingly consistent between observers, which is why they feel true. They are driven mostly by expression and by how much a face resembles a happy, angry, babyish or mature face.

## What your face reliably shows

- **Your current emotion**, through expression.
- **Habitual expressions**, through lines: smile lines, frown lines between the brows, forehead lines.
- **Rest and stress**, partly: tiredness shows around the eyes and in skin tone.
- **Age range, and some aspects of health**, roughly and with many exceptions.

## What face-reading traditions say

Traditions such as Chinese Mian Xiang, Greek physiognomy and Indian Samudrika Shastra assign meanings to each feature. A high, broad forehead is read as a planner's mind; full lips as warmth and generosity; a strong jaw as determination. These meanings are cultural, and are best used as questions to reflect on: *Do I see myself as a planner? Where do I show warmth?*

## What your face does not reveal

Fixed facial structure does not reliably reveal intelligence, honesty, sexuality, political views or destiny. Studies claiming AI can "detect" such traits from faces have been widely criticised for picking up photo style, grooming and expression rather than anything innate.

## A better question to ask

Instead of "what does my face say about me?", try "what does my face say about **how I feel and how I usually look at the world**?" Your resting expression, the lines you are building and the way you hold your face in photos are things you can notice and change.
