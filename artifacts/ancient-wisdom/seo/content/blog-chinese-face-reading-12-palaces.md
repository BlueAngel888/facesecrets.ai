## The three zones

Mian Xiang divides the face into three horizontal parts, the "three stops" (三停):

1. **Upper stop**, hairline to eyebrows: early life, intellect, family background.
2. **Middle stop**, eyebrows to nose tip: middle age, drive, self-reliance.
3. **Lower stop**, below the nose to the chin: later life, relationships, contentment.

## The 12 palaces

The 12 palaces (十二宫) assign life areas to parts of the face. Names and exact positions vary between schools.

| Palace | Location | Traditionally linked to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Life palace | Between the eyebrows | Overall vitality and outlook |
| Siblings | Eyebrows | Siblings, friends, temperament |
| Spouse | Outer corners of the eyes | Partnership and marriage |
| Children | Below the eyes | Children, creativity |
| Wealth | Nose, especially the tip | Earning and keeping money |
| Health | Bridge of the nose | Physical health and resilience |
| Travel | Temples | Movement and change |
| Friends and helpers | Jaw and lower cheeks | Support from others |
| Career | Center of the forehead | Work and reputation |
| Property | Upper eyelids and area between eye and brow | Home and possessions |
| Fortune and virtue | Above the outer brows | Luck and inner peace |
| Parents | Upper forehead | Parents and early support |

## The 100-year map

Mian Xiang also assigns ages to points on the face, beginning at the ears for childhood, moving across the forehead in the teenage years and twenties, through the eyes and nose in the thirties and forties, and down to the mouth and chin in later life. A reader looks at the area linked to a person's current age.

## The five elements

Faces are also classified by the five elements: wood, fire, earth, metal and water, each with a typical shape. See [face shapes explained](/blog/face-shapes-explained).

## How to approach Mian Xiang today

Mian Xiang is a rich cultural system, not a predictive science. Read with curiosity, it can be a structured way to reflect on the areas of your life.
